## LinkedIn Post
Six months ago, I sat down on a Sunday night to "catch up" on my newsletters.

I'm a senior in-house counsel. Deal flow doesn't pause for inbox hygiene. Between term sheets, IC memos, and token side letters, I'd been treating my 23 subscriptions like a guilty pile of unread tabs.

That night, I opened my reader, got through one piece on AI regulation, hit a Bloomberg link, fell into a TechCrunch rabbit hole, and three hours later I had read maybe a sliver of what was queued.

That was the moment I started building Brevis.

Not because newsletters are broken — they aren't. The writers I follow are sharper than ever. The problem is downstream: professionals don't have a triage layer between the firehose and their actual workday.

So I built one. Brevis ingests the newsletters you already pay for, runs them through Claude, and gives you a 5-minute briefing instead of a 90-minute reading list. Bilingual, because half the deals I work on are in Spanish.

Building this while practicing law has been a masterclass in constraints. No 12-hour focus blocks. No "we'll fix it in v2." Every feature has to justify the 30 minutes of evening time it cost me.

The lesson I keep relearning: the right product for busy people gets built by busy people. Not despite the constraint — because of it.

What's the one workflow you keep meaning to fix but never have time to?
