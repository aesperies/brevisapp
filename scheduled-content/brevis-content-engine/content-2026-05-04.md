## LinkedIn Post
Monday morning, 6:47am.

I opened my inbox and counted: 121 unread. Twenty-three of them were newsletters I'd opted into — at some point — because I genuinely wanted what they were selling.

By Friday, I will have read fewer than five.

This isn't a discipline problem. The average professional gets ~121 emails a day and stays subscribed to ~23 newsletters, but reads under 20% of them. The other 80% sit in a quiet pile of self-imposed guilt — too valuable to delete, too long to read, too late to catch up on.

I built Brevis because I was tired of choosing between three bad options:

1. Skim everything and absorb nothing
2. Mark all as read and feel like a fraud
3. Spend an hour at lunch playing catch-up

Now my newsletters get summarized into bullet points by AI, in my language, ready to read in the time it takes my coffee to brew. 30+ minutes saved every morning — but more importantly, the cognitive cost of inbox-as-backlog is gone.

Productivity tip for the week ahead: stop optimizing the *reading* of newsletters. Optimize the *deciding* — what deserves your full attention vs. what's a 60-second briefing. The most underrated professional skill in 2026 isn't speed-reading. It's triage.

What's the one newsletter you actually open every week, and what makes it survive the cut?

— Antonio
