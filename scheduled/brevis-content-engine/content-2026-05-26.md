## LinkedIn Post
I subscribed to a newsletter in Spanish last month — an industry publication out of Mexico City covering gaming regulation across LATAM.

Then one in French about EU AI policy.

Then a German one on Web3 enforcement actions.

Suddenly my morning routine was: switch languages three times before coffee.

That's how the bilingual layer of Brevis got built.

Here's what it does now: I subscribe to newsletters in any source language. Brevis reads them, extracts what actually matters, and delivers one digest — in English or Spanish, whichever mode I'm in that morning.

The math behind the decision: 121 emails hit my inbox on a normal day. 23 of those are newsletters I genuinely wanted to read. I actually open fewer than 20% of them. That's not a discipline problem — it's a translation tax compounded by a context-switching tax.

If you read newsletters across languages, you know the friction. You either skim the original and miss nuance, or you paste paragraphs into a translator and lose the flow of the writer's argument.

Brevis collapses both into one read. Same hand-picked sources. One language. 30+ minutes back in the morning.

Building this in public from Madrid. The bilingual core wasn't a feature — it was the whole reason I started.

If your inbox looks anything like mine: brevisapp.com.
