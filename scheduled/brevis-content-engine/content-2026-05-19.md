## LinkedIn Post
Most professionals I know are subscribed to between 15 and 30 newsletters.

They open less than 20% of them.

This isn't a discipline problem. It's a format mismatch. Newsletters are designed for slow morning reads. Your inbox is designed for fast triage. The two don't meet, so the stack quietly turns into guilt.

Here's the use case Brevis was built for:

You're an in-house lawyer tracking AI regulation. You subscribe to Stanford HAI, Politico AI, Lawfare, IAPP, and a dozen others. The signal is real — but it's buried across 23 issues a week. So you skim subject lines, archive most, and hope you didn't miss the one that mattered.

Brevis reads them for you. Each newsletter gets reduced to 3-5 bullets — the actual claims, decisions, and shifts. You scan a single brief in the morning instead of opening 23 emails. The ones that matter, you click through to the full source.

It's not a productivity hack. It's a different way to consume a format that was never designed for the volume professionals receive today.

The average knowledge worker gets 121 emails per day. Newsletters are a meaningful slice of that for anyone tracking a moving field. Compressing 23 of them into one scannable brief is the most leveraged 30 minutes you'll buy back this week.

Building in public. Live now at brevisapp.com.

What newsletters do you subscribe to but never read? Honest answers only.
