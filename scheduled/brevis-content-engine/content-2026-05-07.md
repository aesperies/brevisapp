## LinkedIn Post
A pattern I've been watching in the newsletter ecosystem this quarter, from the seat of a working professional who lives in their inbox:

Production cost has collapsed. Reading capacity hasn't.

Five years ago, a daily newsletter was a serious commitment. A team, a calendar, a copy editor. Today, a single operator with an LLM and a Beehiiv account can ship five issues a week before lunch. The supply curve has gone vertical.

But the demand side — the human attention curve — is unchanged. The average professional still gets 121 emails a day, still subscribes to ~23 newsletters, still reads less than 20% of what lands. The bottleneck moved from production to reception, and almost no one is building for the reception side.

This creates a structural problem that's only going to get worse:

When AI is on the supply side and humans are on the demand side, the demand side loses by default. Not because readers got worse — because the asymmetry compounds. Every additional AI-generated issue raises the cost of the next human read.

The reading layer has to get smarter too. Not "better filters" in the Gmail sense — that's still asking a human to triage. Real change means flipping the model: AI on both sides. AI reads, ranks, condenses. Human reads the signal, not the substrate.

That's the bet behind Brevis. Not summarization as a feature, but a structural fix to an asymmetry that's about to get much worse.

If you work in a knowledge-heavy field — law, VC, consulting, research — I'd love to know: how many newsletters do you actually open in a given week? And which ones still earn it?
