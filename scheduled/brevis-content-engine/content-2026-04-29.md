## LinkedIn Post
Wednesday founder note.

When I started building Brevis, I assumed the problem was time. The average professional gets 121 emails a day, subscribes to ~23 newsletters, and reads less than 20% of them. Obvious solution: summarize them, give back the time.

That framing turned out to be wrong.

The real cost isn't the minutes spent reading. It's the context-switching tax. Every time you open a newsletter mid-deal — between a redline and an IC memo — your brain pays a small but real price to load and unload that frame. Multiply by 23 subscriptions a day and you've fragmented your deep-work hours into rubble.

What we ended up building wasn't a "newsletter summarizer." It was a single morning digest — every newsletter you care about, condensed by Claude into bullets, delivered before the inbox starts firing. One context load, one read, then the rest of the day belongs to the work that actually moves the deal.

That reframe changed everything: how we prioritize features, how we talk about the value, who we sell to.

Lesson for fellow builders: when the problem statement feels obvious, that's the moment to interrogate it. The boring answer ("save time") usually masks the real answer ("protect attention"). One ships a productivity tool. The other ships a category.

Building in public — would love to hear from other founders: what "obvious" problem statement did you have to rewrite mid-build?
