## LinkedIn Post

Feature spotlight: the "one digest" view.

Here's the problem I kept hitting as in-house counsel: I was subscribed to ~23 newsletters — deal memos, regulatory updates, gaming industry trackers, crypto policy briefings. All signal, in theory. In practice, less than 20% of them ever got opened. The good stuff was buried under 121 emails a day, and by the time I finished triage, the actual reading time was gone.

Brevis was built around one idea: stop reading newsletters. Read *across* them.

Every morning, Brevis ingests your subscribed newsletters and produces a single digest — Claude-generated bullet summaries, grouped by theme, with the original sources one click away. You skim the bullets, you flag the two or three threads worth a deeper read, and you close the tab. The unread pile doesn't follow you around.

Two things I've learned shipping this:

1. The value isn't summarization. It's *prioritization*. Bullets let you triage in seconds what used to take 30+ minutes of scrolling.

2. Bilingual matters more than I expected. Half my market reads English newsletters and thinks in Spanish (or vice versa). A digest that respects both is a different product than one that forces a translation layer.

If you live in a newsletter-heavy role — VC, legal, strategy, research — this is the use case Brevis was built for.
