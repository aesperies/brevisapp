## LinkedIn Post
A trend I keep seeing in every knowledge-work team I talk to: the inbox quietly became the most expensive meeting nobody scheduled.

The numbers are blunt. The average professional now receives 121 emails a day and is subscribed to 23 newsletters. Fewer than 20% of those actually get read. The rest pile up as low-grade guilt — important enough to keep, never urgent enough to open.

For most of the last decade the answer was "manage it better." Filters. Folders. Inbox zero rituals. None of it changed the underlying math, because the problem was never organization. It was volume outrunning attention.

What's shifting in 2026 is the default. Instead of you reading everything to find the 5% that matters, AI reads everything and hands you the 5%. Summarization stopped being a nice-to-have and became the filter layer sitting between you and the firehose.

That's the bet behind Brevis. We turn the newsletters and updates you already subscribe to into one short, skimmable brief — in English or Spanish. People who use it get back 30+ minutes a day. Not by reading faster. By not reading what they didn't need to.
