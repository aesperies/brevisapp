## LinkedIn Post
Six months ago, I made a confession to myself: I was subscribed to 23 newsletters, and I was reading less than 20% of them.

I'm in-house counsel at a venture fund. My job is to know what's happening in gaming, web3, and AI before our portfolio companies do. So I subscribed to everything. Every legal brief, every market dispatch, every founder essay.

The result? Guilt. Every morning I'd open my inbox to 121 emails and feel the pile growing. I'd star three I "had to read later," and later never came.

The honest insight wasn't that I needed more discipline. It was that the format was broken. Newsletters arrive on someone else's schedule, in someone else's structure, and demand my attention at someone else's pace.

So I built Brevis. One bilingual brief, every morning. Everything from my subscriptions, distilled. The signal lives in three sentences. The links are still there if I want to go deeper.

Thirty minutes saved every day. Compounding over a year, that's a week of focused work I get back.

The deeper lesson for any builder: the most valuable products often aren't solving an information problem. They're solving a format problem. The signal already exists. It's just buried in a delivery system designed for the sender, not the reader.

If you're also drowning in subscriptions you can't keep up with, give it a look.

brevisapp.com
