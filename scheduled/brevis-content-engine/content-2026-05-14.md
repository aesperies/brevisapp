## LinkedIn Post

Newsletters quietly became the most important knowledge layer for professionals.

Think about where you actually get industry signal in 2026. Not from blogs — mostly dead. Not from X — algorithmic and fragmented. Not from podcasts — too slow when you need a specific fact. It's newsletters.

VCs read deal-flow digests. Lawyers read regtech and compliance dispatches. Consultants read sector roundups. Operators follow ops-leader bulletins. The independent-publisher boom (Substack, Beehiiv, Ghost, Kit) moved the most valuable analysis off legacy media and straight into your inbox.

Here's the asymmetry nobody talks about: the publishing side scaled. The reading side didn't.

The average professional now subscribes to 23 newsletters and reads less than 20% of them. Not because the content is bad — most of it is the best content they have access to. The format hasn't evolved. Long-form prose, delivered daily, into the same inbox that holds your calendar invites, contract drafts, and HR pings.

That's not a willpower problem. It's a format problem.

That's the gap Brevis fills. Every newsletter becomes 3-5 bullets, summarized by Claude, delivered in the time it takes to finish your coffee. The publishing layer keeps growing; the consumption layer finally has a tool that scales with it.
