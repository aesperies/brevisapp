## LinkedIn Post

Monday productivity audit — the one I run on myself before the week starts:

Open your inbox. Count the newsletters you subscribed to "to stay sharp." Industry average is around 23. Open rate across the category is under 20%. Five out of every six things you opted into this week will sit in your inbox as guilt.

Not because the content is bad. Because the format asks for 10 to 15 minutes of focused attention, and Monday morning doesn't have that kind of time.

Three rules I've landed on, running legal at a venture fund where signal matters and calendar doesn't forgive:

1. Pick three non-negotiables. The newsletters that directly inform a decision you'll make this week — a term, a diligence call, a portfolio update. Three, no more.

2. Batch everything else. Don't read as it arrives. Read on your terms, in one sitting, or not at all. An unfinished subscription is worse than one you never had.

3. Measure read-through, not inbox count. Unsubscribing is a feature, not a failure. The goal isn't to be "caught up." The goal is to act on what you read.

This is, incidentally, why I'm building Brevis on top of my day job — a tool that turns the 20 newsletters you'd skip into one 5-minute morning digest. Bullet summaries, originals one click away, bilingual EN/ES. 30+ minutes a day back.
