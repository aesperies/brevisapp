## LinkedIn Post
A founder lesson I keep relearning:

The features I was most proud of shipping early? Almost nobody used them.

I built Brevis to summarize newsletters for busy professionals. My first instinct was to pack the product with toggles, filters, custom digests, sentiment scoring. Engineering work I could be proud of.

Then I sat with one reader for 30 minutes. An attorney. 121 emails a day, 23 newsletters subscribed, less than 20% actually read. She didn't want more controls. She wanted one screen each morning that told her what mattered before her 9am call.

So I cut. Hard.

The feature shelf got smaller. The product got sharper. And the people I built it with started telling friends.

The lesson isn't "talk to users." Everyone says that. The lesson is: be willing to delete the thing you spent two weeks building because the person actually using it doesn't need it.

Founder ego is the most expensive feature in any product.

Three things I now do before shipping anything at Brevis:

1. Sit with one real reader. Watch them use it. Don't explain.
2. Ask what they'd remove, not what they'd add.
3. If I can't point to a specific person who asked for it, I don't build it.

30+ minutes saved per day for our readers isn't a marketing line. It's the result of saying no to 80% of what I wanted to build.

Building with people beats building for them. Every time.
