## LinkedIn Post
Every Monday used to start the same way: a wall of unread newsletters.

I'm in-house counsel at a gaming and web3 VC fund. To do my job I "have" to follow 23 newsletters — deal flow, regulation, token markets, AI policy. The intent was good. The reality was 121 emails a day and a quiet guilt every time I archived another issue I never opened. Be honest: most of us read less than 20% of what we subscribe to.

So I built the thing I wanted. Brevis reads the newsletters overnight and gives me five lines per source the next morning — the actual signal, in English or Spanish, before my first meeting.

The productivity unlock wasn't "read faster." It was deciding I don't need to read most of it at all. I need to know what changed and what to act on. That's a five-minute scan, not a thirty-minute slog. The 30+ minutes that gives back every morning now go to the work only I can do.

Building this in public as I go. If Monday email is your enemy too, I'd love your take: what's the one newsletter you'd never want summarized — and why?
