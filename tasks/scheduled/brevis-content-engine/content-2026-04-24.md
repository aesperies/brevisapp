## LinkedIn Post
A few months ago, I hit inbox zero on a Friday afternoon and immediately felt defeated.

Not because the inbox was empty — but because I'd spent the better part of two hours doing triage on newsletters I'd enthusiastically subscribed to and never actually read. Stratechery. The Information. Three gaming industry digests. Two crypto regulatory trackers. A handful of AI research summaries. Good signal. Terrible format for a Friday afternoon.

That was the moment Brevis stopped being a side idea and became something I had to build.

The numbers aren't controversial: the average professional receives 121 emails a day, subscribes to 23 newsletters, and reads less than 20% of them. We're drowning in curated information — and the irony is, most of it was curated BY US, FOR US. We don't have an information problem. We have a format problem.

Brevis takes every newsletter in your inbox and turns it into one bullet-point digest. Five minutes. Done. You keep the signal, you lose the scroll.

Three things I've learned building this in public:

1. The people who complain loudest about email overload are the least willing to unsubscribe. They want the information; they just don't want the ritual.

2. "I'll read it on the weekend" is the most optimistic lie professionals tell themselves.

3. Bilingual support matters more than I expected. My Spanish-speaking users treat it as a feature, not a convenience.

If you're a lawyer, VC, consultant, or operator drowning in subscriptions you can't bring yourself to cut — brevisapp.com.

What's the last newsletter you unsubscribed from, and what finally made you do it?
