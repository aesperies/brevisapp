## LinkedIn Post
Last Friday I did something embarrassing.

I scrolled through my inbox to clear the weekend backlog before Monday — and counted 41 unread newsletters from the previous week. Substacks I'd paid for. Trade pubs I'd hand-picked. Industry briefs I told myself I'd "read on the train."

I opened three of them.

This is the dirty secret of every senior professional I know: we subscribe like we're going to read, then we declare bankruptcy every Monday morning. The average pro sits on 23 active newsletter subscriptions and opens fewer than 20% of them. Email volume averages 121 messages per day. The newsletter folder becomes a graveyard of good intent.

The cost isn't the time. It's the asymmetric information you didn't get. The deal flow note, the regulatory shift, the competitor signal — buried under a sender you trusted enough to subscribe to but not enough to open.

This is what I'm building Brevis to solve. AI summarizes your newsletters into bullet points, in English or Spanish, ready before your first coffee. You stop guilt-deleting. You start every Monday with the context you actually paid for.

If you're staring at an unread count this morning that you know you won't get to — that's the gap worth examining. Either the subscription list is feeding you, or it's guilt-tripping you. Rarely both.

Building this in public at brevisapp.com.

What's your unread newsletter count right now? Curious what the room looks like.
